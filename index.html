About

I design systems that have to keep working

I started as a full-stack developer in 2007 and have spent the years since moving steadily toward the architectural end of the problem: how services talk to one another, how data survives the trip between them, and how a team stays productive while replacing something that is already in production.

Most of that work has been in health informatics — national information platforms, disease surveillance, electronic health records — but the engineering has never been specific to it. A fleet management system for law enforcement and a fiber-line portal with live router telemetry pose the same questions: clear domain models, dependable integration, and interfaces people can actually use under pressure.

Alongside the engineering, I lead teams. I've built one from recruitment through delivery, run root-cause sessions across functions, and mentored ministry staff so that systems outlast the people who installed them. I'm now doing a PhD in Complex Systems Engineering, working on data spaces for healthcare: architectures that let organisations exchange clinical data across borders while each keeps authority over its own records.

Selected work

Four systems worth describing

A cross-section rather than a catalogue — clinical, national-scale, operational and telecom.

HAEMOPAL

Electronic health record · Palestinian Ministry of Health with Agenzia Italiana per la Cooperazione allo Sviluppo

A cloud EHR built specifically for congenital bleeding disorders and thalassemia. Clinicians get one secure record per patient covering history, laboratory results and treatment plans, so care stays continuous as patients move between centres. Designed jointly with hematologists and now in use across Palestine.

  • Clinical data modelling
  • Secure cloud access
  • Longitudinal records
  • Stakeholder co-design

National health information platform

Multi-programme integration · Ministry of Health

Consolidating separate health programmes onto shared infrastructure with a common data model — family health, maternal and child health, warehouse and pharmacy — connected through HL7 FHIR and platform APIs, and surfaced to decision-makers as live indicator dashboards rather than monthly spreadsheets.

  • HL7 FHIR
  • REST APIs
  • ETL
  • Data quality
  • Analytics dashboards

Disease surveillance and response

Case-based surveillance and IDSR · national deployment

Case-based surveillance and Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response systems covering the reporting chain from facility to national level, built for outbreak detection and timeliness, with training programmes so the platform stays operable by ministry staff.

  • Event-based data capture
  • Early warning
  • National rollout
  • Capacity building

Fleet management & fiber operations

Law enforcement and telecom · ART Technologies

A full-stack fleet management system for law enforcement, and operations portals for telecom fiber lines with dashboards fed by live CISCO router telemetry. Different domain, same core problem: reliable integration and a view that holds up when someone has to act on it immediately.

  • Full-stack architecture
  • Live telemetry
  • System integration
  • Operational dashboards
